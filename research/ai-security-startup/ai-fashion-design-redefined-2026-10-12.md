# AI fashion designer: redefinition memo

Research as of 12 Oct 2026. Bracketed numbers point to the sources list at the end.

## 1. Verdict

- **Not viable as described.** No company verified does the full chain: culture research, master plan, then automatic buying. Automatic buying has no settled liability rule. Brands use AI mostly for marketing images, not design or sourcing [12][14].
- **Strongest version:** a sourcing and research brief for a designer studio or mid-size brand, with a person approving every supplier and purchase. Funded firms already sell sourcing (Fashinza, Fabriclore, Cala). The hard part is timing. Overseas mill fabric takes 8 to 24 weeks [25], and on-time delivery slipped from 80.5% to 71.3% in one secondhand count [26].
- **Cut:** automatic purchase and payment, and AI judgment on cultural or religious acceptability. Keep the AI as a flag-raiser.

## 2. Redefined idea

**A research and sourcing assistant that turns a design brief into a checked materials plan. A named person approves every cultural call, supplier and purchase.**

It does:
- Finds and cites fabric history and cultural context.
- Lists fabric per garment, with MOQ (minimum order quantity: the smallest order a supplier takes), lead time (weeks from order to delivery) and origin evidence.
- Shortlists suppliers and flags heritage motifs.

It does not:
- Decide what is culturally or religiously acceptable.
- Place orders, pay or contact suppliers without approval.
- Use registered prints or protected regional names without checks. (A geographical indication, or GI, is a legal name tied to a place.)

Human decisions kept in the loop:
- **Cultural review:** a named reviewer signs off any heritage motif. For Indigenous designs, a consent step under a written agreement [21].
- **Supplier approval:** a buyer approves each supplier after origin and forced-labour checks.
- **Purchase approval:** a person approves every purchase order (PO) in version one.

## 4. Risks, ranked

1. **Forced labour.** The EU Forced Labour Regulation (EU) 2024/3015 bans selling or exporting forced-labour goods from 14 Dec 2027, including stock already held [15]. US rules target cotton and apparel. CBP holds shipments until origin is documented. DHS added 43 companies to the UFLPA Entity List (goods from listed firms are held at US borders) on 31 Jul 2026 [16]. *Rule:* get lot-level origin evidence and screen every supplier before payment.
2. **Automated purchase liability and fraud.** US Regulation E (a consumer payments rule, 1978) assumes a human starts each payment. You can stay liable if an agent exceeds its authority. No agent rule was found as of Aug 2026 [17]. Visa and Mastercard agent rules (Visa from 14 Oct 2025) are network rules, not law [18]. EU PSD3 payments law reached final texts on 23 Apr 2026, not yet adopted, and may cover a buyer-side agent [19]. *Rule:* no agent on a company debit account. Use capped virtual cards and a written grant of authority.
3. **Cultural and religious sensitivity.** Most places have no clear legal bar. The risk is government and reputation. In Nov 2020 Mexico's culture minister questioned Isabel Marant over Purepecha-linked motifs. The brand apologised [20]. *Rule:* flag, never decide. Religious norms are not yet researched.
4. **IP for patterns and names.** EU GI rules (Regulation (EU) 2023/2411) apply fully from 1 Dec 2025 and cover textiles and lace [22]. The EU design reform (Regulation (EU) 2024/2822) took first effect 1 May 2025 and recognises surface patterns as products. Its reach to textiles needs legal confirmation [23]. *Rule:* check EUIPO GIView and registered designs before a regional name or print reaches a supplier.
5. **Brands building in-house.** This is market risk, not a legal rule. Inditex (Dec 2025) and H&M (Jul 2025) use AI in customer-facing content [12][14]. No primary sign of in-house design or sourcing AI was found. *Watch:* annual reports and earnings calls.

## 5. First 30 days

- **First customer:** one designer studio or one mid-size brand with one collection a season and a known supplier list. Not a large retailer.
- **Research brief (one page):** garment and season; fabric spec (fibre, weight, width); quantity per colour; target cost; origin rules; heritage sources with link and date; named cultural reviewer; supplier shortlist with MOQ, lead time and certificates; open gaps.
- **First 10 conversations, three questions each:**
  1. How long does it take to find and check a new fabric supplier, and where does the time go?
  2. Who signs before an order goes out, and what would you never let software do?
  3. When a fabric has a heritage source, how do you clear it now, and what does that cost?
- **Weeks 3 to 4:** run the brief on one past collection. Then pilot live, with a person approving each step.

## 6. Kill criteria

These thresholds are my suggestions, not sourced figures.

- Fewer than 3 of the first 10 people agree to a paid pilot within 60 days.
- The customer will not share supplier lists or order history.
- Under half of sampled suppliers can give lot-level origin evidence.
- No named person will own cultural reviews.
- A lawyer says the buyer carries full liability for agent-placed orders. Then pivot to recommend-only, with no ordering.
- A funded platform ships the same chain first. Then pivot to heritage research only.

## 7. Gaps

- Religious norms and religious-sensitivity guidance: not researched.
- Brand AI claims rest on trade press. No brand press release, earnings call or annual report was checked.
- Legal rules come from summaries and the EU Commission page. Primary texts (Reg E, the EU Official Journal, card rulebooks) were not read. EU guideline dates and UFLPA counts conflict across sources.
- Funding figures are mostly aggregator data, and several conflict. Stylumia, EDITED, Trendalytics, Vue.ai and Daydream are left out.
- Alibaba Trade Assurance payment and dispute terms: the page returned a 404.
- MOQ and lead-time figures come from vendor blogs, not surveys.
- Older than 12 months: Heuritech, CLO, Browzwear, Style3D, Fashinza, Fabriclore, Cala, H&M.
- Not covered: trims, tariffs, copyright of AI-made designs, EU CSDDD and German supply-chain law, designer unions outside the US and UK.
- Search used about 8 tool calls. The list is not exhaustive.
