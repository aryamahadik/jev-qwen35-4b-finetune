Three approaches: two match the paper paper §3 · Table 2

The paper checks its answer three independent ways. We ran all three on our runs. Each approach gives the exponents in Nopt ∝ Ca and Dopt ∝ Cb. Brackets are 10th–90th percentiles from refitting on 80% of the runs 100 times, as in the paper. Approaches 2 and 3 land next to the paper. Approach 1 comes out higher: all our runs end at just 4 budgets, so between budgets its "lowest curve" is always an unfinished run, which is the same mid-schedule bias as in section 05.

The headline test: mini-Gopher vs mini-Chinchilla paper §4

The paper's proof: Chinchilla (70B params, 1.4T tokens) beat Gopher (280B params, 300B tokens) on the same compute. We rebuilt that match-up at our scale: same FLOPs, a 4× bigger model on fewer characters vs our compute-optimal model. One reason Kaplan got 0.73: judging models mid-schedule paper App. B · Fig. A1

Kaplan et al. gave every model one long learning-rate schedule and read losses off part-way through. The paper shows that a model judged before its cosine schedule ends looks worse than it is: overshooting the run by more than 25% clearly hurts. At small budgets the big models are judged earliest, so the best size looks too small there, and the fitted exponent comes out too steep. We tested the core effect on one model at one budget.

The code that does it

About 470 lines of Python run the whole experiment, and about 200 more do the three fits. The transformer is a small nanoGPT-style model. Everything Chinchilla-specific is below: compute counting, the matched cosine schedule, the IsoFLOP grid, and the three fits.

Read with care

      
    
        
  • Tiny scale: 29K–1M parameters, compute 10¹²–3×10¹³ FLOPs. The paper used 70M–16B parameters and 6×10¹⁸–3×10²¹ FLOPs.
    • 
        
  • Characters, not word pieces. One word-piece token is about 4 characters, so our "characters per parameter" is about 4× a token count.
    • 
        
  • One seed per run. Learning rate tuned per model width in a pilot; each run keeps its best of up to three batch sizes.
    • 
        
  • The schedule is one cause of Kaplan's 0.73. Later work (Pearce & Song 2024; Porian et al. 2024) points to others too, such as counting only non-embedding parameters and tuning at small scale.
