# Compute Budget ($30 on Modal) and the Benchmark-to-Funding Playbook

Research date: 2026-09-25. Items dated before 2026 are labelled with their date. The dollar figures for "what fits" use Modal's current list prices unless noted.

## 1. What does $30 buy on Modal today (GPU pricing, free tier, startup credits)?

### Takeaway
At Modal's current list prices, $30 buys about 51 T4-hours, 38 L4-hours, 15 L40S-hours, 12 A100-80GB-hours, 7.6 H100-hours, 6.6 H200-hours or 4.8 B200-hours. It is also exactly the Starter plan's free compute allowance, which renews every month. The Modal for Startups programme (up to $25k) needs a $1M+ raise or a partner VC, so a pre-funding student team probably doesn't qualify yet. The academic programme (up to $10k) may be the realistic upgrade.

### Cited Findings
- Modal's per-second GPU prices (Sept 2026): B300 $0.001972/s, B200 $0.001736/s, H200 SXM $0.001261/s, H100 SXM5 $0.001097/s, RTX PRO 6000 $0.000842/s, A100 80GB $0.000694/s, A100 40GB $0.000583/s, L40S $0.000542/s, A10 $0.000306/s, L4 $0.000222/s, T4 $0.000164/s. The page lists "A10" and no separate "A10G". — [Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)
- CPU costs $0.0000131 per core per second (minimum 0.125 cores per container) and memory costs $0.00000222 per GiB per second, both billed on top of the GPU. — [Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)
- The Starter plan is "$0 + compute / month" and includes "$30 / month free compute". The Team plan is $250/month and includes $100/month of free compute. — [Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)
- Credit programmes: "Early-stage startups can get free compute credits"; academics can get "up to $10k free compute credits". — [Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)
- Modal for Startups offers up to $25,000 in credits valid for 12 months, plus engineering and "GTM amplification on launches and raises". Eligibility is being new to Modal and having raised from a Modal VC partner, or over $1M from any fund (Seed to Series A). The credits are a one-time grant. — [Modal Startups page](https://modal.com/startups) via [search summary]; corroborated by aggregators such as [PerkBook](https://perkbook.co/vendor-directory/modal/) and [AI Credit Ladder](https://www.aicreditladder.com/offers/modal-startup-credits). ### Inferences
- The $30 is almost certainly the Starter plan's monthly free allowance, so it recurs. Over a 3-month project the team effectively has about $90, or more if each teammate has their own workspace. Check Modal's terms on multiple accounts first.
- CPU and RAM overhead matters most on cheap GPUs. A container requesting 4 cores and 32 GiB adds about $0.19/hour for CPU plus about $0.26/hour for RAM, roughly $0.44/hour. That is about 75% on top of a T4 and 55% on top of an L4, but only about 11% on an H100. Request minimal CPU and RAM.
- Idle containers, image builds, model downloads and `torch.compile` warm-up all bill GPU time. Cache weights in a Modal Volume and keep container idle timeouts short.
- The Modal Academics credits (up to $10k) are probably the most realistic way to get more compute before any fundraise. Startup credits need about $1M raised or a partner VC.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the exact current eligibility text on modal.com/startups; aggregator summaries conflict on whether VC backing is required.
- Modal's per-container GPU maximum (commonly 8) and the multi-node availability on Starter were not verified in this session.
- I found no Modal-specific guidance on spot or preemptible discounts.

## 2. What training and eval runs fit inside $30?

### Takeaway
Encoder classifiers (ModernBERT) and LoRA/QLoRA fine-tunes of 0.5B–8B models fit comfortably, as do evals of small open models on short-context benchmarks. A classifier or "decision head" costs cents to dollars per run. Single-GPU GRPO on a 1.5B–3B model is feasible but tight. A 32B SFT (s1-style) only just fits on paper. Full-scale reproductions like nanochat (~$52 on Modal), Sky-T1 (~$600) and TRM (~$760) do not fit.

### Cited Findings
- **ModernBERT classifier.** Fine-tuning ModernBERT-base on banking77 (~13,000 customer-service queries, 77 intent classes) for 5 epochs on one NVIDIA L4 took 321 seconds (BERT-base took 1,048 seconds). F1 was 0.93 for ModernBERT versus 0.90 for BERT. The post (Dec 2024/Jan 2025) presents ModernBERT for LLM-routing-style classification. — [Phil Schmid, "Fine-tune classifier with ModernBERT in 2025"](https://www.philschmid.de/fine-tune-modern-bert-in-2025)
- ModernBERT supports an 8,192-token context and is presented as suited to high-throughput uses such as LLM routing. — [ModernBERT paper (arXiv 2412.13663)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.13663); [Phil Schmid](https://www.philschmid.de/fine-tune-modern-bert-in-2025)
- **QLoRA 8B.** Unsloth reportedly cuts Llama-3.1-8B peak VRAM from ~22 GB to ~10 GB and raises throughput from ~3,200 to ~6,500 tokens/s on one H100. This is a secondary summary, not Unsloth's own page. — [Yobitel knowledge base on Unsloth](https://yobitel.com/knowledge-base/unsloth). A small QLoRA fine-tune of Llama 3.1 8B finished in about 34 seconds on one RTX 4090 with 6.6 GB peak memory; this is a toy dataset. — [ComputingForGeeks](https://computingforgeeks.com/fine-tune-llm-unsloth-qlora/)
- Unsloth claims about 2x throughput and 50–55% less VRAM for Llama 3.1 and Mistral, and about 1.7x throughput with 50% less VRAM for Qwen 2.5. — [Yobitel summary](https://yobitel.com/knowledge-base/unsloth); [Qwen docs, Unsloth page](https://qwen.readthedocs.io/en/latest/training/unsloth.html)
- **GRPO (Feb 2025).** Unsloth says GRPO with LoRA/QLoRA needs as little as 7 GB of VRAM, later 5 GB. It says the Qwen2.5-1.5B "aha moment" previously needed 2xA100 (160 GB) and now runs on one small GPU, using 80% less VRAM than HF + FA2. — [Unsloth blog: Train your own R1 reasoning model](https://unsloth.ai/blog/r1-reasoning); [Unsloth RL guide](https://unsloth.ai/docs/get-started/reinforcement-learning-rl-guide)
- **TinyZero (Jan 2025).** It uses veRL and Qwen2.5 base models. 0.5B "fails to learn reasoning", 1.5B fits on one GPU, and 3B+ needs 2 GPUs. The README says "< $30" but gives no training time. The repo is now deprecated in favour of veRL. — [TinyZero README](https://github.com/Jiayi-Pan/TinyZero/blob/main/README.md)
- **Eval throughput.** vLLM on one H100 (offline, 300 requests, 100 input / 600 output tokens) reaches about 7,315 total tokens/s for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B and about 6,488 tokens/s for R1-Distill-Llama-8B. — [DatabaseMart H100 vLLM benchmark](https://www.databasemart.com/blog/vllm-gpu-benchmark-h100)
- LongMemEval has 500 curated questions over long chat histories. The "S" setting is about 115k tokens of context per question. — [LongMemEval paper (arXiv 2410.10813)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10813); the ~115k figure is from [StartupHub on Microsoft Memora](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/ai-research/2026/memora-microsoft-s-ai-memory-upgrade)
- **nanochat.** The current time-to-GPT-2 record is 1.65 hours on 8xH100 (Mar 14, 2026, CORE 0.2626). Karpathy prices this at ~$48 assuming $3/GPU/hour ($24/hour per node), or ~$15 on spot. — [nanochat README](https://github.com/karpathy/nanochat)
- **modded-nanogpt.** The 124M model reaches 3.28 val loss on FineWeb in 1.126 minutes on 8xH100 (record of Aug 6, 2026) using about 400M tokens. The llm.c baseline in May 2024 took 45 minutes and 10B tokens. The GPT-2-Medium (350M) track record is 17.35 minutes (Dec 31, 2025). — [modded-nanogpt README](https://github.com/kellerjordan/modded-nanogpt)
- **s1 (Jan 2025).** s1-32B SFT took 26 minutes on 16 H100s over 1,000 examples. — [s1 paper (arXiv 2501.19393)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19393). Reported cost estimates vary ("~$25", "under $6", widely reported as "under $50"). — [Gonzo ML summary](https://gonzoml.substack.com/p/s1-simple-test-time-scaling)
- **Sky-T1 (Jan 2025).** 19 hours on 8 H100s with DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 offload, about $450 at Lambda pricing. — [NovaSky blog](https://novasky-ai.github.io/posts/sky-t1/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/11/researchers-open-source-sky-t1-a-reasoning-ai-model-that-can-be-trained-for-less-than-450/)
- **TRM (Oct 2025).** Training takes about 2 days on 4 H100s, reported as "less than $500". — [GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20251010-tiny-recursion-model-trm/); [TRM paper (arXiv 2510.04871)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04871)

### Inferences
Budget estimates below are my own arithmetic on Modal prices. Each one states its throughput assumption.
- **ModernBERT-base classifier (decision/routing head).** A banking77-sized run takes about 5.4 minutes on an L4, roughly $0.07 of GPU time. $30 therefore covers hundreds of runs: sweeps, ablations and multiple seeds. ModernBERT-large (~395M parameters vs ~150M) should take about 2–3x as long, which is still well under $1 per run on an L4 or A10. This is the best-value option for a "fast decision/probability-output model that replaces LLM tool calling".
- **Distilling an LLM into a classifier.** The cost is dominated by generating teacher labels, not by training. With a small open teacher (7–8B) on an H100 at ~6,500–7,300 tokens/s, 100k examples of ~200 tokens in and ~20 tokens out is about 22M tokens. Prefill is faster than this decode-heavy figure, so that is roughly an hour or less, about $4. Using a frontier API as the teacher moves the cost off Modal and onto API credits.
- **QLoRA 8B.** At ~6,500 tokens/s (Unsloth on H100), 7.6 H100-hours is about 178M training tokens; at ~3,200 tokens/s (no Unsloth) it is about 88M. For example, a 60k-example function-calling dataset like xlam-function-calling-60k at ~500 tokens per example is about 30M tokens per epoch, so about 1.3 H100-hours or about $5 per epoch with Unsloth. On an L4 or A10, 0.5B–3B LoRA runs are cheaper per run but slower.
- **GRPO.** A 1.5B–3B model on one L40S, A100 or H100 for a few hours is within budget: for example, 5 hours on an A100-80GB is about $12.50. TinyZero-style 3B Countdown on 2xH200 costs about $9.08/hour, so $30 is about 3.3 hours. Whether that reaches the "aha" curve was not verified. RL is the riskiest spend, because rollouts dominate and runs can fail to learn, as 0.5B did in TinyZero.
- **Evals.** BFCL-style single-turn function calling with a 1–8B model under vLLM is short-context and should take well under an hour per model on one GPU, so a few dollars per model. LongMemEval-S is about 500 × 115k ≈ 57.5M input tokens per model per run, dominated by prefill. Expect a few H100-hours per model for 7–8B, and it strains 32k-context models. The oracle or retrieval-based variants are much cheaper. tau-bench-style agent benchmarks also need an LLM user simulator, typically an API model, which adds API spend outside Modal (see Gaps).
- **Speedruns.** One modded-nanogpt 124M run is about 1.1 minutes on 8xH100, roughly $0.60 of pure train time. Compile, warm-up and data loading realistically make it about $2–5 per attempt, so dozens of attempts fit in $30. The record is extremely competitive, though (91+ contributors).
- **Reproductions that do NOT fit in $30 on Modal:** nanochat time-to-GPT-2 (1.65 h × $31.60/h ≈ $52), Sky-T1 (152 H100-h ≈ $600), and TRM (192 H100-h ≈ $758 at Modal H100 prices). s1-32B SFT (≈6.9 H100-h ≈ $27) fits on paper, but it needs 16 GPUs at once, which Starter probably cannot provide, and evaluation of a 32B model would come on top.

### Gaps
- I did not find Unsloth's own first-party tokens/s table for specific GPUs in this session. The 3,200→6,500 tokens/s figure comes from a secondary summary.
- I found no cited wall-clock time for TinyZero's 3B run or for Unsloth GRPO on 1.5B. Hours are not in the README or blog summaries I accessed.
- I found no cited per-run cost or runtime for BFCL, tau-bench or LongMemEval on small open models. The tau-bench user-simulator requirement comes from general knowledge of the benchmark and was not verified in this session.
- vLLM prefill throughput for 115k-token contexts on an H100 was not found. The LongMemEval runtime is a rough extrapolation.

## 3. Precedents: cheap projects that produced notable results and attention

### Takeaway
The most famous cheap results (TinyZero <$30, s1 ~$50, Sky-T1 $450, TRM <$500, nanochat ~$100, modded-nanogpt) all combined three things. They made one crisp claim against a famous reference: "R1-Zero aha", "o1-preview", "beats Gemini 2.5 Pro", "GPT-2". They released open code, weights and data. And they put a dollar figure in the headline. Most came from university or big-lab researchers, not startups. The main documented payoffs are attention, prizes and credibility, not direct VC rounds. HRM/Sapient is the exception, having raised before scrutiny.

### Cited Findings
- **TinyZero (Jan 2025, UC Berkeley, Jiayi Pan).** Reproduced DeepSeek R1-Zero on the Countdown game: "the 3B base LM develops self-verification and search abilities all on its own. You can experience the Ahah moment yourself for < $30." — [Jiayi Pan on X](https://x.com/jiayi_pirate/status/1882839370505621655). Covered widely, e.g. "UC Berkeley researchers managed to replicate DeepSeek AI for only $30". — [Gizmochina](https://www.gizmochina.com/2025/01/31/uc-berkeley-researchers-managed-to-replicate-deepseek-ai-for-only-30/); [Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/tinyzero/). Now deprecated in favour of veRL. — [TinyZero README](https://github.com/Jiayi-Pan/TinyZero/blob/main/README.md)
- **s1 (Jan–Feb 2025, Stanford/UW and others).** SFT of a 32B model on 1,000 examples plus "budget forcing" at test time; training took 26 minutes on 16 H100s. — [arXiv 2501.19393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19393). Published at EMNLP 2025. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1025.pdf)
- **Sky-T1-32B-Preview (Jan 2025, UC Berkeley Sky Computing Lab).** Claimed parity with o1-preview on popular reasoning and coding benchmarks for under $450, fully open-sourced (data, code, weights), and "can be replicated from scratch". — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/11/researchers-open-source-sky-t1-a-reasoning-ai-model-that-can-be-trained-for-less-than-450/); [NovaSky on X](https://x.com/NovaSkyAI/status/1877793041957933347)
- **TRM (Oct 2025, Samsung SAIL Montreal, Alexia Jolicoeur-Martineau).** A 7M-parameter model scoring 45% on ARC-AGI-1 and 8% on ARC-AGI-2, above DeepSeek R1, o3-mini-high and Gemini 2.5 Pro on those tests, trained for under $500. — [GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20251010-tiny-recursion-model-trm/); [arXiv 2510.04871](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04871). It won 1st place ($50k) in the ARC Prize 2025 Paper Awards. — [ARC Prize on X](https://x.com/arcprize/status/1997010155939197295); [ARC Prize 2025 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10904v1)
- **HRM (2025, Sapient Intelligence, Singapore).** A 27M-parameter "brain-inspired" hierarchical model with strong ARC claims. Sapient raised a $22M seed at a valuation above $200M in January 2025, per a secondary source. — [Off the Grid substack](https://offthegridxp.substack.com/p/what-is-sapient-intelligence-hierarchical-reasoning-model-hrm). ARC Prize's own analysis found the hierarchical architecture gave little advantage over a similarly sized transformer. The gains came from an under-documented "outer loop" refinement process, and most performance came from memorising solutions to the evaluation-time tasks. — [ARC Prize: Hidden Drivers of HRM's Performance](https://arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis)
- **nanochat (Oct 2025 to present, Karpathy).** "The best ChatGPT that $100 can buy": about 4 hours on 8xH100 originally, plus a $1,000 tier (d32, ~41.6 hours). — [nanochat Discussion #1](https://github.com/karpathy/nanochat/discussions/1); [Discussion #8](https://github.com/karpathy/nanochat/discussions/8). It now has 58.3k GitHub stars, and the time-to-GPT-2 leaderboard is down to 1.65 hours (~$48) as of Mar 2026. — [nanochat README](https://github.com/karpathy/nanochat)
- **modded-nanogpt speedrun (May 2024 to present, Keller Jordan and community).** The record fell from 45 minutes to 1.126 minutes (Aug 6, 2026), with 91+ contributors, some affiliated with AI companies such as Hiverge and Recursive. — [modded-nanogpt](https://github.com/kellerjordan/modded-nanogpt). A community blog describes record-hunting (WR #82, May 2026). — [Alex Wa's blog](https://djdumpling.github.io/2026/05/27/modded-nanoGPT-WR.html); [LessWrong on WR drops](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/j3gp8tebQiFJqzBgg/how-the-nanogpt-speedrun-wr-dropped-by-20-in-3-months)
- **xLAM-1B "Tiny Giant" (Jul 2024, Salesforce).** A 1B function-calling model reaching 78.94% overall on BFCL, "outperforming models 7x its size, including GPT-3.5 & Claude", built from APIGen synthetic verified data (the 60k dataset was released). — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/salesforce-proves-less-is-more-xlam-1b-tiny-giant-beats-bigger-ai-models); [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/Salesforce/xLAM-1b-fc-r); [SF Research on X](https://x.com/SFResearch/status/1807811770267971984)
- **Puro-2B (2026).** An arXiv paper on a "Poor Lab's" Qwen2-1.5B trained on one RTX 5090 "within $5090". This shows the "budget in the title" framing is still in use. — [arXiv 2608.27370](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27370)

### Inferences
- Pattern: **cost in the headline + famous comparison target + open artifacts + a university or big-lab name** produced the press cycles (TinyZero, s1, Sky-T1 and TRM all had TechCrunch or VentureBeat-tier coverage). A student startup lacks the lab brand, so open artifacts and third-party verification carry more weight.
- The cheapest high-attention results were all **narrow**: Countdown only, ARC only, one leaderboard. A narrow "tool-call decision" benchmark is consistent with that pattern.
- Only HRM/Sapient clearly turned a small-model benchmark claim into VC money, and the ARC Prize later showed the claimed mechanism wasn't the cause. TRM's creator won a $50k prize but stayed at Samsung, with no startup raise found. For the others, the documented payoff is reputation, not funding.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable evidence of jobs or funding resulting directly from TinyZero, s1, Sky-T1 or modded-nanogpt records. The link between Keller Jordan's speedrun and Muon work and a later OpenAI hire is widely discussed but was not verified here.
- Sapient's $22M figure comes from a substack, not a primary funding announcement.
- Quantitative attention metrics (views, stars at launch) for TinyZero, s1 and Sky-T1 were not gathered.

## 4. The benchmark-to-funding playbook: startups that raised on published numbers

### Takeaway
Publishing benchmark numbers is standard in the AI memory and inference-speed categories, but it is rarely the sole reason for a raise. Mem0's $24M rested as much on traction (41k stars, 186M API calls/quarter, an exclusive AWS deal) as on its LoCoMo paper. The clearest "benchmark → round" cases are Poetiq ($45.8M seed right after an ARC-AGI-2 state of the art), Inception ($50M on a ~1,000 tokens/s speed claim) and Engram ($98M on an unverified "100x fewer tokens" claim). I found no startup called "Memorable". The user may mean Mem0, Memories.ai or Engram.

### Cited Findings
**Memory-layer companies**
- **Mem0 (YC).** Raised $24M in Oct 2025: a $3.9M seed led by Kindred plus a $20M Series A led by Basis Set, with Peak XV, YC and the GitHub Fund. TechCrunch cited traction of 41,000 GitHub stars, 13M Python downloads, API calls growing from 35M (Q1 2025) to 186M (Q3 2025, ~30% month over month), 80,000 cloud developers, and status as the "exclusive memory provider for AWS's new Agent SDK". — [TechCrunch, Oct 28 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/28/mem0-raises-24m-from-yc-peak-xv-and-basis-set-to-build-the-memory-layer-for-ai-apps/)
- Mem0's ECAI 2025 paper was a head-to-head of 10 memory approaches on LoCoMo. It claimed 91% lower p95 latency (1.44s vs 17.12s) and 90% lower token cost than full context. Mem0 now self-reports 92.5% on LoCoMo, 94.4% on LongMemEval and 64.1% on BEAM-1M. — [Mem0 research page](https://mem0.ai/research); [Mem0 state of memory 2026](https://mem0.ai/blog/state-of-ai-agent-memory-2026)
- **The Mem0 vs Zep LoCoMo dispute (2025).** Zep claimed 84% on LoCoMo. Mem0's CTO filed a GitHub issue saying a corrected replication gives 58.44%, citing the inclusion of adversarial categories. — [getzep/zep-papers issue #5](https://github.com/getzep/zep-papers/issues/5). Zep's rebuttal (May 6 2025) argued that Mem0 had reported Zep at 65.99% using a wrong user-role setup, timestamps appended to text instead of `created_at`, and sequential rather than parallel search. Zep said its correct score is 75.14% ± 0.17, about 10% above Mem0 Graph (~68%). Zep also noted that a full-context baseline (~73%) beat Mem0's system, that LoCoMo conversations (16k–26k tokens) fit in modern context windows, and that the dataset has missing ground truths and wrong speaker attribution. — [Zep blog: "Lies, damn lies, statistics"](https://blog.getzep.com/lies-damn-lies-statistics-is-mem0-really-sota-in-agent-memory/)
- Analysis of the dispute: scoring relies on an LLM-as-judge prompt that each vendor writes itself, so "the same system … swings many points depending on the judge's wording". — [Atlan: Zep vs Mem0](https://atlan.com/know/zep-vs-mem0/); [dreaming.press on memory benchmark wars](https://dreaming.press/posts/how-to-read-an-agent-memory-benchmark.html)
- **Letta (formerly MemGPT, UC Berkeley).** Raised a $10M seed (Sept 2024) at a reported $70M post-money valuation, led by Felicis. — [BigDATAwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/bigdatawire/this-just-in/letta-emerges-from-stealth-with-10m-to-build-ai-agents-with-advanced-memory/). Letta then showed that a plain filesystem agent on gpt-4o-mini scores 74.0% on LoCoMo, beating Mem0's reported 68.5%, and concluded current memory benchmarks "may not be very meaningful". — [Letta: Is a Filesystem All You Need?](https://www.letta.com/blog/benchmarking-ai-agent-memory/)
- **Zep.** Only $500K of disclosed funding (seed, Apr 2024), per Tracxn. — [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/zep/__poSadJnSfLWHjz05Xi3U5KwnpCMWSU3aDrihLX_8FLs)
- **Supermemory.** A $2.6M seed (Oct 2025) led by Susa Ventures, Browder Capital and SF1.vc, with angels Jeff Dean, Logan Kilpatrick, Dane Knecht (Cloudflare) and David Cramer (Sentry). The founder was 19. — [TechCrunch, Oct 6 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/06/a-19-year-old-nabs-backing-from-google-execs-for-his-ai-memory-startup-supermemory/); [Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2025/10/07/young-founders-supermemory-raises-2-6m-from-cloudflare-and-google-execs/). The benchmark claim came after the raise: "Supermemory is the new State of the art … ~85.86% on LongMemEval" (late Nov 2025). — [Dhravya Shah on X](https://x.com/DhravyaShah/status/1994106663192924546). That state of the art was overtaken quickly: Mastra "Observational Memory" reports 94.87% (gpt-5-mini) and Hindsight reports 91.4%. — [Mastra research](https://mastra.ai/research/observational-memory); [Hindsight arXiv 2512.12818](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12818v1)
- **Memories.ai (ex-Meta founders).** An $8M seed led by Susa (Jul 2025), later an $8M extension bringing the seed to $16M total (Samsung Next, Seedcamp, Fusion Fund, Crane and others). It markets a "Large Visual Memory Model" with claimed state-of-the-art results on K400/K600/K700, UCF101, MSRVTT, MVBench, NextQA and others. — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/135651-memories-ai-raises-8m-seed-extension-for-visual-memory-ai/); [Seedcamp](https://seedcamp.com/views/memories-ai-raises-8m-to-build-human-like-memory-for-ai/); [Memories.ai LVMM blog](https://memories.ai/blog/large-visual-memory-model)
- **Engram (2026).** Emerged from stealth on Jun 23 2026 with a $98M Series A at a $600M valuation, 8 months after founding. Investors include General Catalyst, Kleiner Perkins, Sequoia and Andrej Karpathy; customers include Microsoft, Notion and Harvey. ### Inferences
- **The playbook has four steps.** (1) Pick a benchmark that investors already recognise in the category (LoCoMo/LongMemEval for memory, ARC-AGI for reasoning, tokens/s for inference, BFCL for tool use). (2) Publish a paper or blog with a head-to-head table against named incumbents. (3) Open-source enough for others to rerun it. (4) Pair the number with usage traction. Mem0 is the strongest example of step 4: the TechCrunch article leads with stars, API calls and AWS, not LoCoMo.
- **Seed-stage amounts in memory and routing are modest:** $0.5M (Zep), $2.3M (Not Diamond), $2.6M (Supermemory), $10M (Letta), $16M (Memories.ai), $24M (Mem0). The $45M–$98M rounds went to teams with elite pedigree (Poetiq ex-DeepMind, Engram, Inception's Stanford professor). A student team should benchmark itself against the first group.
- **Benchmark leadership is fleeting.** Supermemory's LongMemEval state of the art (~85.9%) was overtaken within months (Mastra 94.9%, Hindsight 91.4%), and LoCoMo is now largely considered saturated or flawed (full context ~73%, a filesystem agent 74%). A new result on LoCoMo alone would probably draw little attention, and would draw attacks.
- **Poetiq's gap between public and verified scores** (75% public vs 54% semi-private) did not stop the raise, but ARC Prize's independent verification became the reference number. Expect any claim to be re-measured by third parties.
- "Memorable" most likely refers to Mem0 (LoCoMo-driven marketing), Memories.ai (memory in the name, benchmark-led launch) or Engram (2026's big memory raise). I found no company named Memorable.

## 5. What makes a benchmark result credible and newsworthy versus ignored?

### Takeaway
Credibility comes from four things. Results should be on an established benchmark with a hidden or held-out set, or independently verified. Weights, code and eval harness should be open, so others can rerun them within days. Baselines should be strong and fairly configured. And the claim should hold up against obvious simple baselines (full context, filesystem, plain transformer). Newsworthiness comes from a large, easy-to-state gap ("7M params beats Gemini 2.5 Pro", "$30 aha moment", "1,000 tokens/s") on a benchmark the audience already knows.

### Cited Findings
- **Independent verification is decisive in reasoning benchmarks.** ARC Prize verified Poetiq on the semi-private set at 54% ($31/task), versus the self-reported 75% on the public set. — [ARC Prize 2025 results](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-prize-2025-results-analysis); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/poetiq-raises-45-8m-for-ai-meta-system-surpasses-top-llms-on-industry-benchmark-302674571.html)
- **Third-party ablations can deflate architecture claims.** ARC Prize found HRM's hierarchical architecture was not the source of its gains; the outer-loop refinement and task memorisation were. — [ARC Prize HRM analysis](https://arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis)
- **Unreproducible claims backfire publicly.** Reflection 70B could not be reproduced by Artificial Analysis and others, and its creators admitted it. — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/new-open-source-ai-leader-reflection-70bs-performance-questioned-accused-of-fraud)
- **Self-run competitor baselines get challenged.** Zep accused Mem0 of misconfiguring Zep (user roles, timestamps, sequential search). Mem0 accused Zep of including excluded adversarial categories. — [Zep blog](https://blog.getzep.com/lies-damn-lies-statistics-is-mem0-really-sota-in-agent-memory/); [zep-papers issue #5](https://github.com/getzep/zep-papers/issues/5)
- **Simple baselines expose weak benchmarks.** Full context (~73%) and a filesystem agent (74%) beat specialised memory systems on LoCoMo. — [Zep blog](https://blog.getzep.com/lies-damn-lies-statistics-is-mem0-really-sota-in-agent-memory/); [Letta](https://www.letta.com/blog/benchmarking-ai-agent-memory/)
- **LLM-as-judge scoring with vendor-written prompts makes memory benchmarks swing** by many points. — [Atlan](https://atlan.com/know/zep-vs-mem0/)
- **Full openness (data + code + weights, "replicated from scratch") was a core part of Sky-T1's framing** in TechCrunch coverage. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/11/researchers-open-source-sky-t1-a-reasoning-ai-model-that-can-be-trained-for-less-than-450/)
- **Public leaderboards with strict rules** (a fixed target loss, fixed hardware, logged records) turn small improvements into recognisable "world records". — [modded-nanogpt](https://github.com/kellerjordan/modded-nanogpt); [nanochat time-to-GPT-2 leaderboard](https://github.com/karpathy/nanochat)
- **Established external leaderboards confer credibility on tiny models.** xLAM-1B's BFCL placement (78.94%, the only sub-2B model on the board) drove the "Tiny Giant" coverage. — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/salesforce-proves-less-is-more-xlam-1b-tiny-giant-beats-bigger-ai-models); [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/Salesforce/xLAM-1b-fc-r)
- **Vendor-invented or vendor-run evaluations are discounted.** Engram's 100x-fewer-tokens claim was flagged as unverified in coverage. — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319106/20260625/enterprise-ai-token-costs-engram-exits-stealth-98m-claims-100x-cut.htm)
- **New benchmarks are being proposed because the old ones are saturated or flawed** (BEAM; ConvoMem; Penfield's proposal), which shows there is room for a well-designed new benchmark. — [Mem0 benchmark guide](https://mem0.ai/blog/ai-memory-benchmarks-in-2026); [ConvoMem arXiv 2511.10523](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10523v1); [Penfield Labs proposal](https://penfieldlabs.substack.com/p/proposal-a-new-benchmark-for-long)

### Inferences
- For a $30 team, the credible route is: report on an **existing** leaderboard (BFCL for tool-call decisions, or a routing benchmark such as RouterBench), release the weights and eval scripts, include the obvious cheap baselines (a zero-shot 8B LLM, a logistic-regression or embedding classifier, and the incumbent the team claims to beat) run with the incumbent's recommended configuration, and report **latency and cost per decision** alongside accuracy. A speed or cost axis is where a tiny classifier can truly win, as Inception's tokens/s framing and TRM's parameter-count framing show.
- Inventing a new benchmark is acceptable only when it is released openly with a clear critique of existing ones (as Letta and Zep did for LoCoMo). Otherwise a self-invented benchmark reads like the Engram-style unverified claim.
- Put the dollar figure in the headline ("trained for $X on Modal"). It is a proven attention hook (TinyZero, Sky-T1, nanochat, Puro-2B) and fits the team's constraint naturally.
- Plan for adversarial replication. Any incumbent that is beaten, especially a memory vendor, has shown it will publish a rebuttal within weeks (Mem0 vs Zep).

### Gaps
- I found no systematic study of which benchmark announcements convert to funding; the evidence here is case-based.
- BFCL's current (2026) version, its submission process, and whether small classifiers (non-generative) are eligible were not checked.
