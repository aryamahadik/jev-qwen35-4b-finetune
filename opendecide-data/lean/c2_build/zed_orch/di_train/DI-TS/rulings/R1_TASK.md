## Rules in force (A61 a, quoted in short)
1. Licence must be on the allowed list, allow commercial use, and be V: quoted from the creator's official record (repo LICENSE or README, project page) with URL, pinned commit and file sha256. A licence stated only on an HF card, tasksource metadata or a mirror is not V.
2. Text or labels that come through another dataset need that dataset's licence V too.
3. Human text with human labels, or third-party program-generated rows whose label follows by construction. Not allowed: LLM-written or LLM-labelled data, machine translation, distant supervision, engine estimates.
4. TRAIN split only. Never a test split, and never a split that another benchmark uses as its test.
5. Deny-list clean. 6. Not a C1 source, not a dev or eval source here. 7. Sealed-side source-name check PASS (a HIT removes the source).
Bars are never loosened. Rows are dropped, never relabelled. The model is a commercial product.

## Questions. For each id answer "admit" (build rows from its train split), "drop" or "admit_if" (state the exact condition), with a reason of at most 30 words.

R1 LoNLI (github.com/microsoft/LoNLI, MIT, V at commit 7428ca3c). The README calls it a "CheckList-based behavioral testsuite" and "a semi-synthetic large test-bench (363 templates, 363k examples)". Labels come from templates (program, by construction). The release has one data file (data/data_v2.zip) and no train/test split.

R2 SpaceNLI (github.com/kovvalsky/SpaceNLI, MIT, V at commit 8c10e94d). "Natural Language Inference problems specialized for spatial semantics", generated from 160 hand-written problem patterns, 200 samples per pattern by a toy grammar; the paper title is "Evaluating the Consistency of Predicting Inferences In Space". No train split in the release (tasksource packs all 32,000 rows as "train").

R3 FOL-nli (HF tasksource/FOL-nli has train/validation/test; generated by Damien Sileo's gramforge/unigram grammars with a theorem prover, so labels follow by construction). The generator repo github.com/sileod/unigram is MIT (V at commit a8336416), but the dataset's own licence appears only as an apache-2.0 tag on the HF card; the paper is arXiv 2406.11035.

R4 recast megaveridicality and verbnet (JHU Diverse NLI Collection, github.com/decompositional-semantics-initiative/DNC, commit 1aca8f23). The DNC repo has NO LICENSE file. Its per-pair metadata has a corpus-license field: megaveridicality 9,450 train pairs "MIT" (source MegaVeridicality, human inference ratings, recast "automatic"); verbnet 1,398 pairs with a link to the VerbNet 3.0 licence text (automatic recast). Other DNC subsets are already out (factuality 'TODO', puns 'CC BY-NC').

R5 WiCE (github.com/ryokamoi/wice, commit ddeb6c18). LICENSE.md: annotations "released under the terms of ODC-BY"; "The WiCE dataset is based on Wikipedia articles and websites archived at Common Crawl. The majority of text content in Wikipedia is licensed under CC-BY-SA... You are also bound by the Common Crawl terms of use". Labels are human (supported / partially supported / not supported). The evidence is text from the cited web pages.

R6 Perspectrum (github.com/CogComp/perspectrum, commit b42c27b8; data/dataset/license.txt is the full CC BY-SA 4.0 text, README: "Work is published under the Creative Commons license"). Human crowd stance labels (support / undermine), 541 train claims. The claims and perspectives were taken from the debate sites idebate, procon and debatewise (field "source"), which are not datasets. Does rule 2 block it?
