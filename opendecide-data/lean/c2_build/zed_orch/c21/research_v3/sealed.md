## 1. What is new since plan v2

v2 already has: program-only C block, names-only G list, 25% transfer, minimal pairs, two solvers, TPR at fixed FPR, "never F1 alone", always-unsafe baseline, one look. The findings below are what v2 does NOT yet say.

### 1.1 Statistics: resolution, clustering and non-inferiority are the weak joints
- Paired SE for binary outcomes: SE = sqrt(d / n) where d is the fraction of items on which the two models disagree (discordance). Miller gives the general paired form SE_paired = sqrt(SE_A^2 + SE_B^2 - 2 SE_A SE_B Corr) and says to analyse question-level paired differences, to use clustered SEs when questions come in related groups, and to run a power analysis before trusting an eval. His clustered-vs-naive SE ratios on real evals were 3.05 (DROP), 1.88 (MGSM), 1.10 (RACE-H). author-run. https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640v1 (submitted 1 Nov 2024; page read 18 Oct 2026). His worked example needs n about 969 for a 3 pp effect (omega^2 = 1/9, alpha .05, power .8; the parameters are called fictional but reasonable) and he suggests evals of at least 1,000 questions [EDIT refuter 18 Oct 2026: verified on the page; the 1,000 is a suggestion built on that example, not a rule; Table 4 SEs are for Anthropic models].
- Kotawala (28 May 2026, ICML 2026 workshop) shows that paired comparisons are often unresolved at the available N. On the Open LLM Leaderboard v1, 11 of 40 pairs are unresolved at alpha .05 and power .8; on MMLU-Pro 4 of 9 adjacent top-10 pairs, rising to 6 of 9 under real subject clustering. He reports a "resolution ratio" q = N / N* per comparison, and shows that the common shortcut (Cohen's h times (1 - rho)) is off by about a factor of two in close comparisons. author-run (abstract page only). https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30315
- Calibration of tests at n = 500: McNemar chi-square, exact, mid-p, continuity-corrected and paired bootstrap are all within 1.1 pp of nominal alpha; the asymptotic three have median power 0.79 and the exact/continuity versions are about 3 pp conservative. [EDIT refuter 18 Oct 2026: now VERIFIED in the paper body, https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30315 Section 5 / Table 2: all five variants within 1.1 pp of alpha .05 at n=500; median power 0.79 for McNemar, mid-p and bootstrap; exact and continuity-corrected 0.76 and about 3 pp conservative. Caveat: the Table 2 caption says a 55-cell (p, rho, n) grid while the text says n=500 fixed, so the grid is unclear. author-run, simulation. The paper also reports, for MMLU-Pro subject clusters, a median ICC about 0.001 but a median design effect about 1.88 (heavy tailed, only 14 clusters).]
- Connor's (1987) sample-size formula for paired proportions, n = (z_a sqrt(pd) + z_b sqrt(pd - delta^2))^2 / delta^2, with pd = total discordance, delta = difference. Source is a secondary calculator page quoting Connor, Biometrics 43(1) (UNVERIFIED primary; formula text checked on the page, whose worked guidance uses two-sided alpha 0.05 with z=1.96, so the one-sided z=1.645 in C6 below is my choice) : https://metricgate.com/docs/sample-size-crossover-binary/ ; confidence interval for the paired difference should be Newcombe score, not Wald: Newcombe RG, Stat Med 1998;17(22):2635-50, PMID 9839354 (title confirmed on PubMed). [EDIT refuter: the MRC-CBU page https://imaging.mrc-cbu.cam.ac.uk/statswiki/FAQ/BinomialConfidence/2gpp says Wald is too narrow near 0 or 1 and discusses Newcombe/Wilson-based methods, but does NOT mention Tango and does not formally rank methods. Tango's 1998 score interval was not opened: UNVERIFIED; PubMed shows only Tango's 1999 letter on Newcombe's paper (PMID 10611622).]
- ECE is biased and its bootstrap CI undercovers at small n, especially near zero (Sun et al., debiased l2-ECE with proper CI). author-run theory. https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08998v3 (v3, 2 Aug 2025; original 2024). [EDIT refuter: verified wording, 'A Confidence Interval for the l2 Expected Calibration Error': the bootstrap 'undercovers in finite samples, especially for models with a small calibration error'; the debiased estimator targets ECE squared and is itself 'not unbiased' (binning bias); guarantees are asymptotic, simulations at n=100. Note the l2 ECE is a different scale from the usual l1 binned ECE (see Verification).]

### 1.2 Static adversarial sets overstate robustness; 2026 benchmarks go generative
- "The Attacker Moves Second" (10 Oct 2025, USENIX Security 2026): adaptive attacks bypassed 12 published defences, attack success above 90% for most, where most defences had reported near-zero. author-run (the attack authors; whether any co-authored a tested defence is UNVERIFIED: the pages do not name the 12 defences or their authors). https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09023 and https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/nasr [EDIT refuter: USENIX Security 2026 venue verified on the USENIX page, pp. 1467-1486; 14 authors from Anthropic, Google DeepMind, OpenAI, ETH, others.]
- AutoDojo (v3 stamped 28 Sep 2026; CC BY 4.0 paper, MIT code): PIGuard static ASR 0.0% rises to 28.0% under adaptive generation (GPT-4o-mini agent); DataFilter 12.6% to 33.4%; ProtectAI 7.2% to 15.4%; DRIFT 2.6% to 6.4%; PIGuard costs 35.1 points of clean utility (89.5% to 54.4%) [EDIT refuter: this is on DeepSeek-v4-Pro, Appendix C.1, not on the GPT-4o-mini agent used for the ASR numbers]. Action-open tasks are far more vulnerable than fully-specified ones (no defence, static: 87.5% vs 43.3%). Single runs, no variance reported (verified: Section G.3 'We report point estimates throughout'). The 87.5% vs 43.3% figures are AutoDojo's, no defence, STATIC attack, GPT-4o-mini (a third bucket is 63.9%), not AgentDyn's. author-run. https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15057v3 ; code https://github.com/xhOwenMa/AutoDojo (MIT: LICENSE file read 18 Oct 2026; the paper page states CC BY 4.0 but no code licence). The tool needs analyzer and rewriter LLMs (model not named on the repo page): using it at eval time means calling a non-Claude LLM, so it needs an owner ruling.
- AgentDyn (3 Feb 2026): 60 open-ended user tasks, 28 injection tasks, 560 cases; Meta SecAlign 70B ASR 8.98% vs 1.9% on AgentDojo (more than 4x higher); PromptGuard2 keeps 60.00% benign utility but 27.15% ASR; DRIFT 0.83% ASR but 27.09% utility under attack [EDIT refuter: both are the GPT-4o row of Table 3 only; the 1.9% AgentDojo figure appears in the text, and the 8.98 vs 1.9 comparison crosses two different benchmarks]; authors conclude nearly all defences are insecure or over-defensive. author-run. https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03117v1
- Meaning for K/X: an ASR measured on a fixed attack list is a lower bound on exposure and must be paired with a benign-block (over-defence) number at the same threshold.

### 1.3 Validity audits of agent/guard benchmarks (the gold, not the model, is often wrong)
- Agentic Benchmark Checklist (ABC), task validity and outcome validity: tau-bench lets a trivial agent pass 38% of tasks; SWE-bench Verified lets wrong patches pass; of 10 audited benchmarks 7 have outcome-validity flaws, 7 task-validity flaws, all have reporting gaps; flaws can mis-state agent performance by up to 100% relative. author-run audit. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02825 [EDIT refuter 18 Oct 2026: now VERIFIED in the v5 full text (https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02825v5, v5 dated 7 Aug 2025): 'This trivial agent achieves a 38% success rate and outperforms a GPT-4o-based agent' (trivial = empty responses counted as success); 'we assessed ten popular agentic benchmarks'; seven outcome-validity and seven task-validity; 'none satisfies every reporting criterion'.]
- "Safety, or Just Capability?" (30 Jul 2026): on R-Judge 52.7% of items are unsafe, so "always unsafe" scores F1 0.690 and beats five of 21 real models (F1 0.30-0.57); balanced accuracy correlates with MMLU at rho +0.71 but specificity only +0.16; similar-capability models still differ in safety (permutation p 0.004 / <0.001 [EDIT refuter: these are organisation-group permutation tests for misalignment and jailbreak after capability adjustment, not the capability-correlation p-values; rho +0.71 / +0.16 are from the n=20 R-Judge panel]); recommends full confusion structure, drop standalone F1, bound grader variance. independent. https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28685v1
- Safety-benchmark consistency (11 Apr 2026): 12 models over AgentHarm, AgentDojo, InjecAgent, ODCV-Bench give Kendall's W 0.10 (p 0.94); only 4 of 40 surveyed benchmarks have a joint safety-utility metric or an over-refusal test; 28 of 40 score by rule. independent. https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16282
- Platinum benchmarks (Vendrow et al., Feb 2025): revising 15 popular benchmarks for label errors/ambiguity changes what frontier models appear to fail. independent. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.03461 (the MMLU-Redux error rate I looked for was not in the pages I read).

### 1.4 Time-splits and canaries are weaker than v2 implies; construction beats detection
- "Test of Time" (26 Aug 2025, v4 13 May 2026, ACL 2026): the post-cutoff accuracy drop is highly sensitive to how questions are built; an LLM rewrite of LiveCodeBench problems largely removed the drop; a missing drop does not prove no contamination [EDIT refuter: this last clause is my inference, NOT the authors' explicit claim; their abstract says the signal is 'highly sensitive to how benchmark questions are constructed']. author-run. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00072
- Rephrased and translated samples evade n-gram overlap; a 13B model trained on rephrased benchmark items reaches GPT-4-level scores on MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval. author-run. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.04850 (Nov 2023). The authors' own advice: fresh one-time exams.
- Canary strings: depend on trainers honouring them; BIG-bench canary was reproduced by a GPT-4 base model; a missing canary is weak evidence. independent commentary/survey. https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/kSmHMoaLKGcGgyWzs/big-bench-canary-contamination-in-gpt-4 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.17521 (survey, v2 29 Sep 2025; its dynamic-benchmark criteria were not on the abstract page).
- Contamination taxonomy by defeated mitigation (29 Aug 2026): five types (direct, derivative, temporal, distributional, acquired). "Holding out a private test set closes the first alone." Low coder agreement (median kappa 0.21; per-variable linear-weighted kappa over 29 documents, range 0.00-0.35; pooled 0.46 attributed to chance correction) so treat the taxonomy as a checklist, not a measurement. author-run. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29463
- Programmatic generation is now common: FuncBenchGen (ICLR 2026; hidden function-dependency DAG; difficulty by graph size, depth, connected distractors; code location UNVERIFIED) https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26553 ; Reasoning Gym (Apache-2.0, 100+ procedural generators, seeded, `score_answer`) https://github.com/open-thought/reasoning-gym . Template-instance variance is real: GSM-Symbolic found noticeable variance across instantiations of the same template https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05229v2 (Oct 2024); GSM1k found drops of up to 8% (NeurIPS version; 13% in v1) on a fresh matched set https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/53384f2090c6a5cac952c598fd67992f-Abstract.html (author-run).
- Held-out-by-repo design: SWE-Bench Pro uses a public set (731), a held-out set (858, different repos, kept private) and a commercial set (276, private codebases), so a public-vs-held-out gap is a leak signal. author-run. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.16941 (the copyleft "deterrent" claim is unproven per the search summary). Same idea as our G / transfer split.
- Adversarial filtering of eval items biases against the model used to filter and makes rankings unstable. author-run. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.08181

### 1.5 How guard and judge benchmarks report (2025-2026)
- Balanced accuracy = mean of TPR and TNR is the headline in LLM-AggreFact; threshold is the fixed midpoint, not tuned per dataset on dev. author-run. https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10774v2
- TraceSafe-Bench (8 Apr 2026, COLM 2026): reports TPR (unsafe accuracy), TNR (benign accuracy) and balanced average accuracy; 1,000+ execution instances, 12 risk categories; guard efficacy tracks structured-data competence (rho 0.79) not jailbreak robustness. Paper licence CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (NC). author-run. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07223
- LlamaFirewall (6 May 2025): recall at 1% FPR on an in-house out-of-distribution jailbreak set, English: PromptGuard 1 21.2%, PromptGuard 2 22M 88.7%, 86M 97.5%. AgentDojo: no guardrail ASR 17.6% / utility 47.7%; PromptGuard2-86M 7.5% / 47.0%; AlignmentCheck 2.9% / 43.1%; combined 1.75% / 42.7%. vendor-run (Meta). https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03574v1
- GuardianAgentBench (23 Jul 2026): 580 scenarios, accuracy and FPR; guardrail recovers 19.9% of failures at 0.5% FPR. author-run, abstract page only. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20982
- AgentDoG 1.5 (28 May 2026): its training data are LLM-generated and from the same ATBench generation family it is tested on (ATBench 32,787 raw pairs; GPT-5.4 writes rationales); the visible text states no train/test dedup. 4B: R-Judge acc 92.2 F1 92.7; ATBench acc 72.4 F1 74.3. author-run. https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29801v1 . Consequence: ATBench is a home-field set for AgentDoG; and the model breaks our "no LLM-generated data" rule, so it is a comparator only.
- ATBench itself: 1,000 trajectories, 503 safe / 497 unsafe, human audit. author-run. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02022
- DynaGuard (Sep 2025, ICLR 2026): evaluates on unseen user-defined policies (DynaBench); LlamaGuard3 13.1 and WildGuard 20.9 on DynaBench vs DynaGuard-8B 72.5 [EDIT refuter 18 Oct 2026: VERIFIED in Table 3 of https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02563 (author-run, F1 %); 72.5 is the non-CoT mode, 73.1 with CoT; test rules are 'mutually exclusive' with train rules; ICLR 2026 venue UNVERIFIED (page shows v3, Oct 2025); DynaGuard's training dialogues and labels are LLM-generated (GPT-4o-mini/4.1-mini, GPT-4o/Gemini), so like AgentDoG it is comparator-only for us]. Precedent for a "unseen policy" transfer axis.

### 1.6 Coding-agent safety tests: scoring by state, not by refusal
- Saber (v2 24 Aug 2026, CC BY 4.0 paper): 716 executable tasks in Docker; seeds from earlier benchmarks and CVEs expanded by templates plus LLM-assisted details; 95.9% (687/716) kept their risk mechanism after execution and manual review; harmful violation rate 54.7% best (Claude Opus 4.6) to 84.7%; scores final workspace state and action trace, tracks incapability separately so over-refusal is not mistaken for safety. author-run. https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01317v2 . Not usable as a source for us (LLM-assisted, CVE seeds) but the scoring lessons apply to C-PERM/C-INJ.

### 1.7 Holdout reuse
- Reusable holdout / Thresholdout and the Ladder leaderboard (ICML 2015): repeated adaptive looks at a test set overfit it; Ladder only moves the reported best when a new score is significantly better. https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02629 (Dwork et al., 'Generalization in Adaptive Data Analysis and Holdout Reuse', NIPS-era arXiv version, 8 Jun 2015; [EDIT refuter: this arXiv paper is NOT the Science 2015 paper; the Science paper is a separate shorter article that I did not open, and the page never names Thresholdout]) , https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04585 (Blum and Hardt, 16 Feb 2015; abstract confirms leaderboard overfitting but NOT the update rule, which I did not open: UNVERIFIED). Our "one look" rule is the strongest form of this; the delta is what to do if a second look is ever needed (section 5, item 11).

## 2. Corrections to v2

C1. Power lines assume independent items. v2 "C total SE about 0.8-1.0 pp, transfer about 1.6-1.8 pp" are exactly sqrt(d/n) with d about 0.25 and no clustering (DERIVED: sqrt(.25/3700) = 0.82; sqrt(.25/800) = 1.77). Items come from templates and 40% sit in minimal pairs, so they are clustered. With a design effect (deff) of 1.5 the SE grows by 1.22x, with deff 2 by 1.41x: C total 1.0-1.2 pp, transfer 2.2-2.5 pp, C-SEC 150 about 5-6 pp. Miller's real clustered ratios up to 3x show deff 2 is not exotic. [EDIT refuter: the ratios are SE ratios (DROP 3.05, MGSM 1.88, RACE-H 1.10), i.e. deff = ratio squared = 9.3, 3.5 and 1.2; Kotawala's MMLU-Pro subject clusters give a median deff about 1.88. So planning deff 1.5 (SE ratio 1.22) is optimistic against both sources; deff 2 needs about 1,540 transfer items for SE <= 1.8 pp at d 0.25 (DERIVED). Also the two resolution thresholds are inconsistent: transfer 2.0 pp at 925 items implies deff 1.5 at d 0.25, but C total 1.2 pp at 3,700 implies deff about 2.2.] Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640v1 . The planning deff is my assumption (UNVERIFIED for our generators).

C2. G FLOOR as written is close to a coin flip. "C2.1 - C2 LB > -1.0 pp" is a non-inferiority test. If the true difference is 0 and the paired SE is 0.5 pp, a one-sided 95% LB passes only if the observed difference exceeds -1.0 + 1.645 x 0.5 = -0.18 pp, i.e. about 64% pass rate (DERIVED), so a harmless model fails the ship floor about 36% of the time. To pass at 90% power when the truth is 0 you need SE <= 1.0 / (1.645 + 1.282) = 0.34 pp (DERIVED). Alpha and sidedness of "LB" are not stated in v2. Also, for G the generalisation unit is the source, not the item: with 20-25 sources the between-source spread of the paired difference dominates. If that spread is 1.5 pp the SE is 1.5 / sqrt(20) = 0.34 pp (assumption; UNVERIFIED), so "20 sources x 500 items" buys little over "25 sources x 300".

C3. TPR at 0.4% FPR cannot be measured on a sub-block. "Gate 450" with perhaps 200-250 negatives has at most one score in the 0.4% tail. Rule of three (zero false positives in n negatives gives a 95% upper bound of about 3/n; standard result, Hanley and Lippman-Hand 1983, not re-verified) needs 750 negatives to bound FPR at 0.4% and 300 for 1%. A stable TPR at a quantile threshold wants about 5 expected false positives: 1,250 negatives for 0.4%, 500 for 1% (DERIVED heuristic). Carlini et al. justify reporting TPR at low FPR (<0.1%) https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03570 [EDIT refuter: verified on the abstract page, but this is a MEMBERSHIP-INFERENCE paper (LiRA), so it is a transferred principle, not evidence about guard models; the 'it needs many negatives' cost is my derivation, not a quoted finding]. Also: if the threshold is picked from the sealed negatives, the reported TPR is optimistic. LLM-AggreFact fixes the threshold ahead of time https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10774v2 ; LlamaFirewall sets scanner thresholds to a fixed 3% utility cost https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03574v1 [EDIT refuter: verified; this is a rule-based threshold, and the page does not show it was frozen on a separate dev split, so it supports 'state the threshold rule', not 'freeze on non-sealed data'].

C4. ECE <= 3.5% is untestable per sub-block. For a perfectly calibrated model with accuracy about 0.85 and 10 equal-mass bins, the expected binned ECE is about 0.8 x sqrt(p(1-p)/n_bin) (DERIVED): about 4.2 pp at n=450, 3.2 pp at n=800, 1.5 pp at n=3,700. So n=450 has a noise floor above the gate. Pool, use a debiased estimator and Brier/NLL for sub-blocks. https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08998v3

C5. "Options randomised" is one draw of the order. Selection bias in multiple-choice is mostly option-ID token bias plus a smaller position effect (Zheng et al., ICLR 2024, 20 LLMs, 3 benchmarks; author-run). https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03882 . A single random order adds noise and can hide a systematic label bias. Our readout is one prefill, so all cyclic shifts are cheap.

C6. K/X "attack success <= max(C2+2 pp, 3%)" at static strings is not a robustness claim (section 1.2). Detecting a 2 pp worsening at base ASR about 3% (pd about 0.04) with 80% power one-sided alpha .05 needs about 620 attack items by Connor (DERIVED); v2 does not give the K/X count here ("as in v1").

C7. "Two Haiku blind reads; items both mark unanswerable are dropped." This is a model-based filter on test items. Adversarial/model-based filtering biases against the filter model and destabilises rankings (Phang et al.). The danger here is mild (Haiku is not a comparator) but dropping items an LLM finds unclear selects for LLM-surface-easy items. Gold is program-derived, so unclear items should go to a program trace check, not be silently dropped. Weak-to-medium.

C8. n-gram/sentence methods in G13 are not enough for program-vs-program leakage. Rephrase evasion (section 1.4) is about text; for generators the equivalent is same-skeleton-different-surface (same template with new nouns). The reverse pre-check in v2 hashes "templates and wording pools", which is right, but the method list should include a normalised-skeleton match (strip names, numbers, identifiers, paths) and nearest-neighbour embedding check on the sealed side only.

C9. "Time-split public sets cover that ground as Tier 2" is fine, but note the Test-of-Time result: a flat pre/post cutoff curve is not evidence of cleanliness, and the true cutoffs of Qwen3.5-4B and Jev are UNVERIFIED. Time-split Tier 2 sets are supporting, not clean.

### 5.2 Changes
