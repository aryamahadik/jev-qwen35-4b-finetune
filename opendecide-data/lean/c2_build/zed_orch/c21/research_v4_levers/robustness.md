## Five findings to read before the table

1. The plan's optional symmetric-KL consistency term is weakly supported, and two 2025-26 comparisons point the same way. Seleznyov et al. (arXiv 2508.11383, 15 Aug 2025, author-run, 8 open models, 52 tasks): LoRA with a Jensen-Shannon consistency loss had higher spread and lower accuracy than plain format-augmented LoRA for all models (appendix), and plain format-augmented LoRA "barely improved robustness" (spread lower than few-shot on 1 of 8 models). "Prompt-Robust Language Models: Which Training Strategies Work?" (arXiv 2609.01217, 1 Sep 2026, author-run; Llama3.2-1B, Llama3.1-8B, Qwen3-0.6B, Qwen3-8B): consistency and contrastive objectives "mostly matched or beaten" by simple batching; the best-to-worst template gap stayed 40-57% of performance. Plan consequence: keep the default "plain copies" branch for permutations; do not spend a trainer change on a KL term for format invariance. Invariance comes mostly from data coverage and batch scheduling (R8 below).
2. Static attack numbers will overstate real robustness. "The Attacker Moves Second" (arXiv 2510.09023, Oct 2025, author-run, 12 defences) found adaptive attacks above 90% success on most defences that reported near zero under static attacks; AgentDojo static ASR as low as 1% became above 95% for two defences. Our K/X gate is static-attack ASR, so every robustness claim must say "static". The P-I adaptive sweep in the plan is the right counterweight; it is small, so keep its published drop in the same table as the static number.
3. Guard-style detectors fail by content-keying, with high confidence. "Confidently Wrong" (arXiv 2606.22659, 21 Jun 2026, single-author, 3 public detectors, independent of the detector vendors): on missed attacks confidence stays 0.99-1.00 while false-negative rate ranges 0.01-0.97 across five attack shifts; one detector scored pooled ECE 0.06 but 0.91 on attacks alone. This supports the plan's trap-slice ECE and motivates R5.
4. Long inputs are an attack surface even when the payload is intact. Overflip (arXiv 2609.15013, Sep 2026, author-run): repeating a prompt flipped 5 of 9 lightweight guards from malicious to benign (flip rates 8-92%, first flips at about 2.6K-9.4K tokens). Prompt Overflow (arXiv 2605.23196, May 2026, author-run): payload pieces interleaved with natural prose bypassed Prompt Guard 2 (86M) above 99.5% under sliding-window max-pooling. These are 512-token encoder guards, not 4B decoders that read the whole input in one prefill, so the effect on us is untested; they define what to test (R6, R7).
5. Nothing found is large for average accuracy. Robustness levers protect tails (attack classes, odd formats, long inputs, non-English), and the honest expected effect on OHS-5 G point estimates is near zero. They matter for the K/X, long-input and "unseen policy/tool" claims.

## Budget note that applies to the whole table (DERIVED)

Plan projection: about $18-28 for 76-95K rows at mean 800 tokens per row, cap $30. Cost scales roughly with tokens x steps, so if every row-adding lever below were added on top of the plan, tokens would grow about 20-28% (R1 wrapper pairs about +6%, R6 tail at 8% of rows about +17% or at 5% about +11%, R4 about +3%, R12 about +1%), i.e. about +$4-8, which can break the cap at the high end of the projection. Implement the row-adding levers by SUBSTITUTION (swap an equal number of replay or general rows), not addition, or let the A37(k) stop-and-ask rule decide after the measured s/step. Levers marked "replace" in the cost column add no rows.

## Plan-adjustment findings (not levers, but they change what the plan should say)

1. Symmetric-KL for order or format invariance: default to plain copies (finding 1). Keep flip rate as a reported metric.
2. Batch Calibration is the best of five prompt-robustness methods when there is no distribution shift, adds near-zero cost, and is "fragile under class imbalance", where it had the largest accuracy drop (Seleznyov, above). That supports the plan keeping the letter-prior offset OPTIONAL and fixed from dev, not estimated online from traffic; production traffic base rates differ from the 40-60% training balance.
3. Template ensembles and sensitivity-aware decoding cost multiple reads and lost accuracy or ranked last in the same study; both fail the latency target. Rejected, not listed as levers.
4. Every robustness line in a product claim should read "static attack set" and carry the P-I adaptive number beside it (finding 2).
5. PIDS-Bench found hard-negative augmentation removed over-defense on curated stress inputs but left it largely intact on externally sourced benign text, and that enlarging the pool did not close the gap; the plan already hedges this with the P1 vendored-repo text. Nothing new, but it argues for keeping the T41c real-text slice conditional on its 8.5 PASS.
