# C2.1 research v4, angle "accuracy" (Sun 18 Oct 2026)

Scope: general and agentic ACCURACY levers for small (1-8B) fine-tuned classifiers/judges/decision models, 2025-2026, that are NOT already in the C2.1 plan v3.1 (training-side sections 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 8) or in the measured/dropped lists (ALREADY_TESTED_C1_OPT.md, THINK_MODE_MEASURED.md). Web research only; nothing downloaded, no model run, no sealed/ohs/Jev-output path opened.

Tags: author-run = the paper's own authors measured it; independent = a third party measured it; vendor = company-run; UNVERIFIED = I could not confirm the claim or the number from the primary page. "DERIVED" = my arithmetic from stated inputs, shown in the row or in the notes.

## 0. Bottom line (read this first)

1. The honest message for the "<= 3 pp behind Jev on G" goal: nothing in this angle is a big accuracy lever. Published 2025-2026 evidence says LoRA variants, merge variants and selection methods mostly land within 1-2% of a tuned vanilla LoRA, and several lose to random selection. The levers below are worth about 0.1-0.6 pp each, and are NOT additive in any provable way. Pooled, a realistic reading is +0.3 to +1.2 pp on C-transfer families and 0.0 to +0.5 pp on G (DERIVED from the per-row ranges, assuming overlap and diminishing returns; not a forecast).
2. Resolution problem (DERIVED): the plan's G SE is 0.40 pp (section 3 block). A lever worth +0.3 pp cannot be seen on G by itself (needs about 2 x SE = 0.8 pp). So these levers must be chosen on prior evidence plus CPU checks, not "tested one by one on G".
3. One-shot problem (DERIVED): the plan is ONE Modal run (<= $30). Any lever that changes the loss, optimiser or architecture has no A/B inside that run. Cost basis: plan says about $18-28 for 76-95K rows x 2 epochs, so about $0.12-0.15 per 1K row-epochs (18/152K, 28/190K). A 10K-row, 1-epoch micro-A/B arm costs about $1.2-1.5 and a pair about $2.4-3.0 (ignores boot/startup overhead, so a floor). That spend is "training" under the Modal rule but needs an R1-type owner OK because it sits outside "one run". Levers are therefore grouped as: (A) free, no change to the trained function; (B) needs a micro-A/B; (C) owner-reserved.
4. Highest value per risk (my ranking): L1 gold-bug audit, L2 AFLite-style row filter as the family "fix iteration" tool, L5 last-K checkpoint averaging with a moderate end learning rate, L3 error-neighbourhood mutation, L4 pair-aware batching + pair loss, L11 Flat-LoRA (only with an A/B). Cheapest "do not lose accuracy by accident" checks: L13, L22, L23, L24.
5. Closest published analogue found: "LLM2Jev" (Microsoft, 1 Oct 2026, author-run), a Qwen3.5-4B option-probability decision model fine-tuned with LoRA r16. Its lessons are folded in below (L22, L23, L14): LoRA beat full fine-tuning at 4B by 2.2-3.5 points; narrow in-domain data lowered the general benchmark by 5.2 points after one epoch while validation loss kept falling; KL anchors protected output format, not accuracy.

## 4. Notes on evidence quality and what I could not verify

- Most "LoRA variant" and "selection" results are on 0.6B-13B models with math/code/instruction tasks; I found none on one-token readout classifiers except LLM2Jev, and none that fine-tune Qwen3.5-4B for agent-safety decisions.
- Not retrieved or not readable: per-method tables of arXiv 2601.22708; numbers behind the Ortho-LoRA, PairCFR, XTF and WSM-for-SFT claims; WSM's SFT details; the 2026 SlimPajama mixing comparison (secondary snippet only).
- LoRA capacity (DERIVED, why rank is not a lever): LLM2Jev gives 32.5M trainable parameters for Qwen3.5-4B LoRA r16 on all linear layers. Thinking Machines cites about 2 bits/parameter of capacity, so about 6.5 x 10^7 bits. A readout dataset of up to 125K rows with at most about 2 bits of label each is at most about 2.5 x 10^5 bits, roughly 260 times less. Capacity does not bind, so raising rank (owner-only in the plan) should add about 0.
- Seed noise (Lee et al., 3-run std +-0.33 to +-0.79 on 1B math) is of the same order as most lever effects here, which is why per-lever claims are ranges centred near zero.
- LLM2Jev's title as returned by the fetch tool is "LLM2Jev: LLMs Are Already Jev-Style Decision Models"; I used only the public paper's text and no Jev output.
- All sources fetched or searched on 18 Oct 2026. Search snippets for items marked UNVERIFIED were not confirmed on the primary page.

## Verification

Refuter pass, Sun 18 Oct 2026. Every cited primary page was opened (abstract pages, plus HTML full text where the abstract was not enough). Verdict tags sit in the first cell of each lever row. Counts: 23 lever rows = 17 KEPT, 5 UNVERIFIED, 1 REFUTED.

Rule used: KEPT = cited source says what the row says (or the row's own range is honest about being a guess) and the effect range is not overstated. ### REFUTED (1)
- L22: the LLM2Jev evidence (arXiv 2610.02076, v3 revised 13 Oct 2026, Li and Wagle) is misquoted. (a) LoRA vs full fine-tuning at 4B is +3.9 (lambda 1), +3.0 (0.1), +2.6 (0), tie at lambda 0.01; not "2.2-3.5 at every anchor weight". p<=0.02 is for 4B lambda 1; p=0.04 is the 0.6B result. (b) The calibration warning is reversed for LoRA: 4B LoRA ECE 0.050 vs 0.057 training-free (better, not worse); full fine-tune 0.070; Reason-9k 0.067 (no 0.046 anywhere). (c) The paper never ablates the legal-token normaliser alone, so the +0.0 to +0.3 effect has no source. (d) One seed per configuration (seed 20260922), no multi-seed.
