## 0. Bottom line (read this first)

1. Two different budgets, two different answers. Server time is 14.1 ms p50 (SGLang 0.5.20, H100, batch 1) but our measured end-to-end p50 was 137 ms (laptop to us-west, round 1, server was 32 ms then). About 100 ms of the e2e is NOT the GPU. The plan's e2e target is 75 to 85 ms. The biggest e2e levers are therefore network and client side (S7, S8, S9), not kernels.
2. Kernel and engine levers are small. All answer-preserving server-side levers together (S1, S3, S4, S5, S6) are worth roughly 1 to 4 ms of the 14.1 ms (DERIVED, 7 to 28%), and about 0 to 5% at saturation. Reason: at 557 tokens per request (DERIVED from Jev's list price and measured cost) the H100 is already near the compute floor of about 9 ms (DERIVED, 3.9B non-embedding parameters assumed, 50% utilisation of tensor cores), so there is only about 5 ms of launch and overhead to remove at batch 1 and none at batch 8 and above.
3. Cost is a utilisation problem, not a kernel problem. C1 cost is $0.0137 per 1K decisions at 100% GPU use, $0.069 at 20%, against Jev's measured $0.0234. The levers that move cost most are: a cheaper GPU tier for low load (S11, lowers the request rate at which we beat Jev's price from about 47 req/s to about 9 to 23 req/s, DERIVED), fewer FLOPs per decision (S19 multi-field readout, S17 cascade, plus the in-plan prefix cache), exact-match caching (S14) and a lower-priced batch tier (S15).
4. Nothing here needs a bigger teacher, other-LLM data, eval-set training or a different base. Levers S17 to S22 change the model or training format and are owner-reserved.
5. Several prior beliefs got weaker: weight-only INT4/INT8, FP8 KV cache, chunked prefill, early exit, AMD/TPU/Neuron all look like no-gain or no-go for a prefill-only 4B readout (section 4).
6. A competitor to know about, not a lever: Intern-Decision 0.8B/2B/4B (Shanghai AI Lab / InternLM, arXiv 2610.22131, Oct 2026, author-run) is a Qwen3.5-based one-prefill decision family. Its 4B scores 80.55 vs Jev 73.35 on its own "Typed Decision" suite and 44 ms on an RTX 4090 with the native Hugging Face backend, BF16, SDPA attention (289 tokens, 3 fields; the paper does not say eager). Our 14.1 ms H100 SGLang number is a different stack and hardware, so no like-for-like claim.

Facts about Qwen3.5-4B used below (HF config.json, vendor, fetched 18 Oct 2026, https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B/blob/main/config.json): hidden 2560, vocab 248,320, 32 layers = 24 linear-attention (GDN) + 8 full-attention, KV heads 4, head_dim 256, GDN value heads 32 with 128x128 state, vision_config present, tied embeddings.
DERIVED from it: KV cache = 32 KB per token (26 MB for an 800-token request); GDN recurrent state = about 50 MB per request or per cached prefix snapshot in fp32 (25 MB in bf16); LM head = 636M parameters = 1.27 GB in bf16.

## 6. In-plan items where new evidence changes the risk (not counted as levers)

- **Prefix caching of the policy card on a GDN hybrid is not free.** SGLang caches a recurrent-state snapshot at the prefix boundary (about 50 MB each in fp32, DERIVED), at 64-token page granularity with `--mamba-radix-cache-strategy extra_buffer --page-size 64` (SGLang cookbook, vendor). PR 27220 (author-run, bot-closed 30 Sep 2026, fix not merged) measured radix ON up to 17.8x worse mean TTFT than `--disable-radix-cache` on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B at 1k/1k load (default `no_buffer` disables overlap scheduling; 12% worse even with the fix; 4096-token prefill-only +3.6%). Issue 21158 reports zero hit rate on a repeated identical prompt (the scheduler keeps the last token out of the prefix). The plan reorders to static-first only when under 60% of gate-call tokens are already shared; it should add an A/B of radix ON (extra_buffer) vs OFF on od-l4b, on the real request mix, before anyone counts a saving. Capacity: about 200 snapshots plus running requests on a 24 GB L4 (DERIVED), so many customer policy cards may need the host-memory tier (HiCache). Tail-Replay (arXiv 2608.30310) targets this problem: it caches the full-attention KV and omits recurrent-state checkpoints, rebuilding the linear-attention state by replaying a short recent suffix on a hit (abstract read by the refuter; no numbers read).
- **Batch composition changes probabilities.** SGLang's decision docs report up to 0.07 probability movement between cold and cached requests and across batch compositions (argmax unchanged in their checks). The 99.5% parity bar and the per-type temperatures should be re-checked under mixed batches, not only batch 1.
- **Competitor floor on price.** A generic FP8 Qwen3.5-4B costs $0.03 per M input tokens on DeepInfra (3 Apr 2026, vendor), about $0.0167 per 1K requests at 557 tokens (DERIVED). Any "cheaper than Jev" claim should state utilisation (already in plan section 6).
